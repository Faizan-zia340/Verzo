## Short answer: no — if it helps people

If you have ever typed a prompt into an AI writer and wondered whether Google will punish the result, you are not alone. Google does penalise low-quality content — and much AI content is low quality.

But the fear is aimed at the wrong thing. Google does not penalise content for being written by AI — it penalises unhelpful content, no matter who wrote it.

## What Google actually said

Google's clearest statement is its Search Central post, [Google Search's guidance about AI-generated content](https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2023/02/google-search-and-ai-content) (8 February 2023, Danny Sullivan and Chris Nelson). Three points matter most:

**Quality matters, not the method.** Google's systems "aim to reward original, high-quality content" showing E-E-A-T: expertise, experience, authoritativeness and trustworthiness. Its "focus on the quality of content, rather than how content is produced" has guided its results for years.

**Appropriate AI use is not against the guidelines.** The post's FAQ says so plainly. What is against them is using automation "with the primary purpose of manipulating ranking in search results." AI also gives no ranking boost — "It doesn't give content any special gains. It's just content."

**Mass production is the problem.** In March 2024 Google added a spam policy, **scaled content abuse**: many pages "for the primary purpose of manipulating search rankings and not helping users." It names generative AI explicitly.

Google also asks three questions — **Who, How and Why**: who created it, how, and why.

## What actually hurts your SEO

- **Thin pages at scale.** Hundreds of near-identical pages saying nothing new.
- **No original value.** Repeating the top ten results in different words gives Google no reason to rank it.
- **Fake expertise.** Confident advice on health, money or law with no real knowledge behind it.
- **No human review.** Publishing raw AI output unread — AI still invents facts and drifts off topic.
- **Keyword stuffing and duplication.** Repeating the keyword in every heading, or publishing the same article twice under a new title.

Every one is a content problem, not a production problem — a human can write thin content too.

## What does not hurt your SEO

AI content is safe when it answers a real reader's question, is edited by a human who checks the facts, and is original — your angle, your examples, your data. ## FAQ

**Does Google penalise AI-generated content?**
No — not for being AI. Appropriate use of AI is not against Google's guidelines. It penalises content made mainly to manipulate rankings, whoever wrote it.

**Can AI content rank number one?**
Yes, if it is the most helpful result. Google says AI "doesn't give content any special gains. It's just content." Ranking depends on quality and E-E-A-T — not on how the draft was produced.

**Do I have to disclose that AI helped write my content?**
Google's Who, How and Why guidance says it helps readers to know how content was produced — guidance, not a requirement. This blog is honest about AI help.
